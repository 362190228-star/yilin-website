# Visual Reference

## Direction

Use the attached Aeline-style references as inspiration for Yilin Cai's personal portfolio, but translate the system into a personal UX designer brand rather than copying the template. The desired feeling is professional, fresh, spirited, and AI-native.

## What To Borrow

- Large, breathable sky-blue hero atmosphere with a clear sense of openness and confidence.
- Soft photographic or image-led presence in the first viewport, preferably using Yilin's own portrait or project visuals.
- Floating modular cards that preview work, metrics, project screenshots, or AI design themes.
- High-contrast black/white information panels mixed with pale neutral cards.
- Electric lime accents for important actions, active states, small badges, and visual punctuation.
- Rounded but controlled surfaces: friendly and contemporary, not overly bubbly.
- Clean sans typography with generous scale contrast and tight hierarchy.
- A sense of depth through perspective, layering, and soft shadows rather than heavy decoration.

## What To Avoid

- Do not make it look like a generic AI consulting or SaaS template.
- Do not use fake client logos, pricing widgets, or business-dashboard content that does not fit a personal portfolio.
- Do not turn the site into an ordinary resume page.
- Do not overuse identical cards or repeated tiny uppercase section labels.
- Do not let the bright lime overpower the portfolio content.
- Do not make cards so decorative that project substance becomes secondary.

## Portfolio Translation

### Hero

The hero can keep the current sky concept, but should become more specific to Yilin:

- Replace generic "Hello" emphasis with a clearer personal positioning statement.
- Use the portrait as an atmospheric anchor rather than a plain profile photo card.
- Float 4-7 mini cards around the hero to preview strengths: AI Agent UX, interaction design, design systems, user research, internship map, selected project snapshots.
- Use lime for one primary action and small highlight dots, not for large decorative blocks everywhere.

### About

Use a strong centered statement with embedded icon dots or small visual badges, similar to the reference's bold "global consulting partner" line, but rewrite it for a designer:

- Emphasize AI product experience, interaction design thinking, and reliable execution.
- Keep supporting profile details secondary and scannable.
- Use photo/project cards and concise proof points instead of resume-like lists where possible.

### Internships

The internship section can become a more polished "growth map":

- Keep the map metaphor, but reduce literal resume density.
- Use floating cards for company, role, project focus, and design contribution.
- Let each internship answer: what product, what problem, what design value.

### Projects

Projects should feel like curated case-study tiles, not placeholders:

- Large image-first cards with clear titles and tags.
- One featured case can use a darker card for emphasis.
- Use lime chips sparingly for AI / UX / interaction keywords.
- Avoid repeated same-size cards if the work has different weight.

## Visual Tokens To Explore Later

- Sky blue field: vivid, clean, optimistic.
- Accent lime: electric and precise, used as signal.
- Ink: near-black for authority and contrast.
- Surface: white and very light cool gray.
- Image treatment: soft depth, cloud/air atmosphere, real portfolio imagery.
- Motion: cards gently float or settle into perspective; reduced-motion users get static composition.

## Design Principle

The reference should make the portfolio feel more alive and premium, but every visual move must still help a hiring viewer understand Yilin's strengths faster.
